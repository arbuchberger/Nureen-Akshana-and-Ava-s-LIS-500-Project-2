Dr. Joy Buolamwini on Machine Learning

Algorithmic Justice, Accountability, and Inclusive AI

Dr. Joy Buolamwini, a computer scientist and founder of the Algorithmic Justice League, argues that machine learning systems do not exist in a vacuum. Instead, they reflect the data, design choices, and power structures that create them. Her point of view emphasizes that “accuracy” alone is not enough, models can be highly accurate on the groups they see most and still fail—and harm—those they underrepresent. Drawing from her research on facial analysis, she shows how skewed training data can lead to dramatically higher error rates for darker-skinned women compared with lighter-skinned men, underscoring that bias is a measurable technical risk and a civil-rights issue. Pertaining to our project, we greatly support creating more equal and just algorithms for those who are underrepresented in our society, in particular, those who are mute. It highly focuses on making current inaccessible spaces into accessible spaces for mute people without an American Sign Language interpreter, giving a "voice" to the voiceless.

Additionally, Buolamwini’s prescription is a lifecycle approach to accountability: audit models before and after deployment, document datasets and training decisions, measure performance across demographic slices, and give impacted communities a seat at the table. She advocates for transparency, meaningful contestability when systems make mistakes, and regulatory guardrails that match the stakes of real-world use. In short, her POV reframes ML progress as not just making models bigger or faster, but making them more just through inclusive data, rigorous evaluation, clear disclosure, and consequences when systems cause harm. To implement this aspect into our project, we have tested and altered it a plethora of times with different hand positions for it to recognize hand shape, instead of focusing mainly on background.

Machine learning isn’t neutral by default; it inherits our choices. If we want fair outcomes, we have to design for them.
